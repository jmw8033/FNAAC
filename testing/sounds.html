WHAT ACTUALLY MAKES THESE WORK

Metal is two inharmonic partials over a low body. Ring frequencies that are simple multiples of each other read as a musical note; ones that are not read as a struck object. Every metallic sound in the game is a sine around 50–60 Hz with two squares somewhere near 900 and 1300 that are deliberately not related.

Layer delays do more than filters. A door being destroyed is three impacts at 0, 380 and 760 ms with the frame tearing at 1080. Spread in time is what separates “an event” from “a noise”.

Slide the frequency, always. A tone that holds pitch sounds synthetic. Every body hit in the game slides down 30–40% over its length; rising slides read as something going up, which is how the duct climb tells you which way he is moving without a word of interface.

Envelope shape carries the material. (1-t)^3 is a hard strike, (1-t)^1.3 is a dull thud, sin(tπ) is a swell with no attack at all — use that one for anything that should feel like it was already happening.

Mix quieter than feels right. Everything in the game sits under 0.45 gain per layer. Sounds that clip lose their shape, and the ones that frighten people are the ones they lean in to hear.
